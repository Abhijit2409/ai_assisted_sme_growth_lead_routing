# Acquisition intelligence and KPIs

> **A hypothetical framework for a fictional bank, built on entirely synthetic data.** Every rule, threshold, SLA and
> capacity limit is a design proposal awaiting business approval. No figure here describes, benchmarks or recommends
> the practice of any real Canadian bank. Market opportunity is an external public-aggregate signal; everything about
> leads, relationship managers and outcomes is generated by a program. Nothing in this layer supports a causal claim
> about conversion, revenue, profitability or customer experience, and no financial or revenue estimate is produced.

## The questions this layer answers

> Where should the bank focus SME acquisition effort, which prospects need action, and where are leads being delayed,
> lost, or constrained by capacity?

It connects six things that already existed separately — Canadian SME market opportunity, synthetic CRM acquisition
activity, funnel performance, lead priority, operational RM routing, SLA performance and RM capacity — into five
marts and one executive KPI set. It adds no new scoring, changes no opportunity, priority or routing weight, and
tunes nothing using conversion outcomes.

Reporting cutoff: **2026-05-31**, the last day of the declared synthetic operating period (assumption A-018).

## The five marts, and what each is for

| Mart | Grain | Primary key | Answers |
|---|---|---|---|
| `mart_acquisition_market_focus` | one row per CMA at the planning snapshot, plus one explicit row for leads whose geography did not resolve | `cma_key` | Where should effort go? |
| `mart_acquisition_funnel_monthly` | reporting month × CMA × channel × SME segment × industry grouping | `(reporting_month, cma_key, channel_code, sme_segment, industry_grouping)` | Where are leads delayed or lost? |
| `mart_rm_acquisition_performance_monthly` | relationship manager × reporting month | `(rm_id, reporting_month)` | Where is capacity the constraint? |
| `mart_acquisition_action_queue` | one row per currently actionable lead | `lead_id` | Which prospects need action today? |
| `mart_acquisition_kpi_snapshot` | one row per reporting cutoff date | `reporting_cutoff_date` | How is acquisition performing overall? |

## How double counting is prevented

Five history tables fan out badly against a lead: status history reaches **12** rows for a single lead, SLA events
**27**, activities **19**, assignments **6** and routing decisions **6**. Joining any of them straight into a mart
would multiply its lead counts.

Every mart therefore reads one intermediate model, `int_acq__lead_spine`, which is **one row per lead** and is where
each of those sources is collapsed exactly once. A lead can only be counted once, by construction. The executive
snapshot goes further: each KPI is reduced to a single value at its own correct grain before the single values are
combined, so no fact table is ever joined to another fact table at row level.

Three tests enforce this: the funnel, the market-focus mart and the executive snapshot must each reconcile to the
source lead count, and no lead may appear in more than one funnel cell.

## The maturity rule

A lead captured last week has not failed to convert — it has not had time to. Counting it as a failure would make
every recent month look bad and would make the conversion rate drift with intake volume.

**A cohort is mature for conversion only 90 days after capture**, and mature for qualification after 30 days. 95 per
cent of observed conversions happen within 93 days of capture and 90 per cent within 76, so 90 days is the round
figure that covers almost all of them. Immature cohorts are excluded from those denominators entirely and published
in their own column (`valid_leads_immature`), so the exclusion is visible rather than hidden. A test asserts that
mature plus immature always equals the valid population.

## The planning snapshot is never applied to history

The market opportunity score describes a market **now**. Attaching today's score to a lead captured nine months ago
would imply the bank knew it at the time. The monthly funnel mart therefore carries no opportunity score, rank,
classification or pillar, and a test asserts that none of those columns ever appears there. Market opportunity lives
only in `mart_acquisition_market_focus`, which is explicitly a current planning snapshot.

## What the data says

### Where to focus

**9 of 41** CMAs sit in the framework's strongest external opportunity class under the balanced planning scenario.
They hold about a quarter of the leads that resolved to a CMA. Six CMAs cannot be scored at all; they are reported as
scale-only or no-evidence markets rather than as zero opportunity, so a gap in the evidence is never read as a weak
market.

### Where leads are delayed or lost

Of **17,400** leads captured in the operating year:

| Stage | Result | Reading |
|---|---|---|
| Assignment | 99.5% of valid leads | Routing places almost everything it is given |
| First contact | 90.1% of assigned leads | Owned leads are generally worked |
| Qualification | 27.8% of mature contacted leads | The first real narrowing |
| Conversion | **12.5%** of mature valid leads | The end-to-end result |

**The funnel does not lose its volume at intake or routing. It loses it after the first conversation.** That is where
an operational improvement would have to be aimed; adding more leads at the top would not change it.

First-contact SLA breaches run at **13.2%** of settled clocks — roughly one lead in eight misses the response
promise. Only clocks that are stopped or already past target count, so a clock still legitimately running is never
scored as a miss.

### What needs action now

**1,408** leads need an operational step at the cutoff. The largest single action is continuing qualification, which
matches the funnel finding. Terminal leads are excluded unless they still sit in an unresolved duplicate group that
somebody must clear.

The queue is ranked deterministically: escalation first, then SLA urgency, then priority tier, then priority score,
then lead age, then a stable identifier. Two runs produce the same order, so the list can be worked top-down and
handed over without ambiguity.

### Where capacity constrains acquisition

Most of the network has headroom; a small number of relationship managers are at or above the declared warning
level. Capacity is not the binding constraint on this synthetic year.

## Routing concentration: the operational risk to decide on

The capacity-aware routing policy makes **zero** hard-cap breaches. That is the intended result, and it is worth
stating plainly that it is not the whole story:

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| Share of recommendations to the busiest decile of RMs | **72.31%** |
| Workload Gini across all 140 RMs | **0.773** |
| Recommendations to the single busiest RM | **3,216** |
| RMs receiving no recommendation at all | **8** |

**Hard-cap compliance does not automatically create a balanced workload.** The routing preference rules reward the
relationship manager with the most headroom, and in a lightly loaded year that is repeatedly the same person. The
capacity ledger prevents anyone being pushed past the limit; it does not spread the work.

This is surfaced as a risk for the business to decide on. **The routing rules are deliberately unchanged at this
stage** — changing preference weights to improve a concentration statistic, after seeing the statistic, is exactly
the kind of outcome-driven tuning this project avoids. The options (reweighting territory continuity against
headroom, adding an explicit fairness constraint, or accepting the concentration as correct given the capacity
evidence) are a business decision, not a data-engineering one.

## Running it

```powershell
powershell -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File .\run_analytical_pipeline.ps1 -Scope AcquisitionIntelligence
```

The scope confirms the upstream layers are current, loads the two configuration seeds, builds the spine and the five
marts, runs the tagged dbt tests, runs the analysis, runs the independent validator and checks determinism. The
layer is also included in `Full`.

## Assumptions and limitations

- **Everything about the CRM is synthetic.** Leads, prospects, relationship managers, assignments, SLA clocks,
  capacity and outcomes are generated. Rates describe the generator's behaviour, not a real bank's.
- **Market opportunity is an external signal only.** It excludes bank market share, branch presence, competition,
  credit risk, acquisition cost and profitability.
- **Capacity is a declared model**, not a measured workload, and is attributed to a CMA by the RM's home territory,
  which understates coverage by RMs who also serve neighbouring territories.
- **The cohort basis is capture month.** A lead belongs to the month it arrived and is followed wherever it went;
  this is not an activity-in-month view, and assignments are attributed to the month of assignment while conversion
  may happen months later.
- **Conversion is only reported on mature cohorts.** The three most recent months carry a large immature population
  by construction.
- **Routing exceptions cannot be attributed to an individual** because a queued decision has no recommended RM, so
  they are attributed to the lead's territory and named accordingly.
- **Historical outcomes are descriptive.** Nothing in this layer feeds back into the opportunity, priority or routing
  rules, and no weight was changed on the basis of any result here.
- **This is not a dashboard and not a model.** There is no machine learning, no forecast, and no revenue estimate.
