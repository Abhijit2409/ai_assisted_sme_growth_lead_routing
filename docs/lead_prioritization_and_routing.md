# Lead prioritization and capacity-aware RM routing

> **A hypothetical framework for a fictional bank, evaluated on entirely synthetic data.** Every rule, weight, band
> threshold, SLA, capacity limit and specialist requirement below is a design proposal awaiting business approval. None
> of it describes, benchmarks or recommends the practice of any real Canadian bank. Every lead, prospect, relationship
> manager (RM), territory, assignment and capacity snapshot is generated by a program. Comparisons against the synthetic
> historical assignments are rule-adherence comparisons, routing-policy counterfactuals, operational-exposure measures
> and assignment-quality proxies. **Nothing here is evidence of a causal effect on conversion, revenue, profitability or
> customer experience**, and no such claim may be derived from it.

## The question

> Which eligible SME leads should receive attention first, and which available RM or controlled queue should receive
> each lead based on territory, specialist requirements, capacity and operational rules?

The framework answers it transparently: every score, every eligibility decision, every preference contribution and every
queue result is published with the raw value, the transformation, the weight, the contribution and the rule that
produced it. There is no model to interpret — only declared rules applied to as-of evidence.

It contains no machine-learning model, conversion prediction, propensity score, revenue forecast, profitability estimate,
optimization solver, production CRM integration, dashboard or executive KPI mart.

## Decision-time grain

**One row per lead per routing-decision timestamp.** The timestamp is published as `as_of_timestamp`, and every feature
must be available at or before it.

| Decision trigger | Decisions | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| `historical_routing_decision` | 18,230 | Every timestamp in the synthetic routing log: initial assignment (15,975), reassignment (1,860), controlled override (252) and queue release (143). Only the identifier, lead, timestamp and decision type are read from that log. |
| `intake_routability_review` | 1,547 | One constructed decision point per lead the routing engine never saw, at the lead's first status change away from `NEW`. Without these, duplicates, invalid, merged, withdrawn and held leads would be silently absent and the eligibility taxonomy could never be exercised. |
| **Total** | **19,777** | over 17,400 leads |

The as-of lead status is **recomputed** from the status history, using changes strictly before the decision instant plus
changes stamped exactly at it whose event is not `ASSIGNMENT_CREATED` (that event is caused by the decision being
replayed). It is not copied from the routing log: the two differ on 13 decisions where an assignment was released at the
same instant, which is itself the evidence that the value is independently derived.

## Leakage prohibition, and how it is proved

None of the following may ever enter a priority score, an eligibility decision, a preference score or a recommendation:
later activities, later statuses, later assignments, later capacity snapshots, later SLA outcomes, qualification
outcome, opportunity creation, conversion, closed-lost outcome, the historical selected RM, the historical recommended
RM, generator scenario labels or ground-truth expected outcomes. `fct_opportunity_outcome` is never a scoring or routing
input, and nothing under `data/synthetic/crm_ground_truth/` is read by any model.

Four independent proofs, all run on every build:

1. **Manifest lineage.** `19_validate_lead_prioritization_and_routing.py` walks `depends_on.nodes` transitively for all
   thirteen scoring and routing models and asserts that no opportunity-outcome relation, no ground-truth path and
   neither `mart_routing_recommendation_static` nor `mart_routing_evaluation` appears anywhere upstream. A model could not
   evade this by aliasing columns.
2. **Column-level read check.** The compiled SQL of every scoring model is parsed: the historical routing log is located,
   the alias bound to it in the `FROM` clause is read, and every `alias.column` reference is collected. Exactly one
   model reads the log, and it reads exactly `routing_decision_id`, `lead_id`, `decided_at` and `decision_type`.
3. **Independent recomputation.** The validator rebuilds the decision population, every feature, the eligibility, the
   score, the band, the candidate eligibility, the preference, the ranking and the queue outcome in pandas from the
   Parquet sources and the declared seeds, reading no model SQL, and compares row by row.
4. **Mutation tests.** Fourteen injected faults, including "ground truth used in scoring", "opportunity outcome used in
   scoring" and "historical selected RM used as a feature", are each detected by their specific check.

Two deliberate design choices support this:

- `surviving_lead_id` is **not** used to decide the `merged` eligibility outcome, because it is written when the merge
  happens and would be future information at an earlier decision. Only the as-of status and the as-of duplicate group
  count.
- The prior owner used for the exclusion rule and the continuity preference is the RM owning the lead immediately
  **before** the decision, taken from an assignment that started strictly earlier. It is never the assignment the
  decision itself created, which is asserted on the assignment identifier rather than the RM identifier.

effective weight, contribution and whether the declared neutral value was used. The score is rebuilt from the ten
contributions in a fixed component order, so it is identical on every build whatever the thread count.

**The market-context cap.** 0.65 x 0.12 = **0.078**, so external geography can move a lead score by at most 7.8 points
out of 100. It can never dominate. Tier 3 scale-only leads and leads with no market context take the **declared neutral
value 50, never zero**; 2,453 scored decisions use it, and a dedicated test asserts that a Tier 3 lead is never scored
as zero opportunity and is never excluded because of its tier.

## Routing

### Mandatory eligibility, separate from preference

Five lead-level gates stop a decision before any RM is considered; nine candidate-level rules then filter RMs. **An
ineligible RM can never be recommended, whatever its preference score** — preference is only computed on candidates that
have already passed every mandatory rule.

| Rule | Scope | Passes when | Queue on failure |
|---|---|---|---|
| REL-01 | lead | Priority eligibility is `score_eligible` and no override suppressed the score | Q-MAN |
| REL-02 | lead | Not in an unresolved duplicate group at the decision instant | Q-DUP |
| REL-03 | lead | Not in `VALIDATION_PENDING` | Q-DRM |
| REL-04 | lead | Geography resolved to a CMA or a provincial remainder | Q-OOF |
| REL-05 | lead | A territory is resolved | Q-TER |
| REL-06 | candidate | Employed and active on the decision date | |
| REL-07 | candidate | Availability in the as-of snapshot is not leave or unavailable; **not evaluated when no snapshot exists** | |
| REL-08 | candidate | Effective territory coverage on the decision date, or the TER-10 national fallback | Q-TER |
| REL-09 | candidate | Remote and national pools are served only by their own coverage; the national fallback is a second stage | Q-NSP |
| REL-10 | candidate | For a mandatory-specialist industry, an effective **primary** specialization in it | Q-NSP |
| REL-11 | candidate | An explicitly stated service language is supported; **language is never inferred** | Q-LNG |
| REL-12 | candidate | A capacity snapshot exists at or before the decision instant | Q-CAP |
| REL-13 | candidate | Utilization strictly below the hard limit of 1.00 | Q-CAP |
| REL-14 | candidate | Not the prior owner on a reassignment, and has not declined this lead before | |

**Effective dates.** `valid_to` and `employment_end_date` are **exclusive** bounds. The synthetic source settles this,
not the model: a leaver's last capacity snapshot is strictly before their employment end date, their territory
`valid_to` equals that same date, and no assignment is made on or after either. The validator asserts this from the
source.

**Candidate universe.** An RM enters it when it holds a territory relationship to the lead's territory in any effective
period, and additionally, on a mandatory-specialist lead, when it holds a primary specialization in that industry. REL-08
and REL-10 are then evaluated per candidate, so a lapsed or not-yet-effective relationship is a visible failure. RMs with
no relationship to the territory at all are structurally out of scope and are counted in `structurally_excluded_rm_count`
rather than enumerated. The result is 210,053 candidate rows over 17,991 evaluated decisions, of which 149,195 are
eligible.

**The national specialist pool (TER-10).** A mandatory-specialist lead with no eligible regional candidate may be served
by an RM holding effective coverage of the national specialist territory and the required primary specialization. This
is a **second stage**: it is evaluated only after the regional stage, so a national-pool RM never competes with an
eligible regional RM on preference. 96 decisions use it; 18 still find no specialist at all and are held in Q-NSP.

### Capacity as of the decision

For every candidate the framework takes the **most recent valid capacity snapshot with `snapshot_as_of_utc` at or before
the decision instant**. A later snapshot is never used, and the validator asserts both that no snapshot is dated after
its decision and that no more recent valid snapshot existed. Every candidate publishes the snapshot id, its timestamp,
weighted load, capacity limit, utilization, warning status, hard-limit status, remaining capacity and exception
eligibility.

When **no** valid snapshot exists, the candidate fails REL-12 and the decision is held in a controlled queue rather than
guessed. REL-07 is deliberately not evaluated in that case: availability is unknown, not bad, so the reason given to the
queue owner is the missing snapshot. The 12 decisions on 2025-06-01 — the day before the first nightly snapshot — are
exactly this case and are held in Q-CAP.

**No ordinary recommendation is ever made at or above the hard limit of 1.00.** Zero occurrences, asserted by a dbt test,
by the independent validator and by a mutation test.

| Preference | Weight | Normalization |
|---|---|---|
| RPF-01 territory coverage role | 0.20 | primary 100, backup or national pool 50 |
| RPF-02 specialist fit | 0.18 | mandatory met 100, preferred specialist held 100, generalist on a preferred industry 40, no specialist need 70 |
| RPF-03 segment fit | 0.12 | match 100, mismatch 40 |
| RPF-04 continuity with a valid prior owner | 0.10 | valid prior owner 100, no prior owner 50, other 0; never applied on a reassignment |

## Two recommendations, and why both exist

The framework publishes two recommendations over the same decisions. Keeping them apart is the point.

| | `mart_routing_recommendation_static` | `mart_routing_recommendation_operational` |
|---|---|---|
| What it answers | Which RM best fits this lead on the declared rules, considered on its own? | Which RM should actually get this lead, given everyone already assigned before it? |
| Capacity | Read, never consumed. Every decision sees the same observed snapshot. | Read and consumed. Each assignment adds workload to the chosen RM for every later decision. |
| Order | None. Decisions are independent. | A single declared order: timestamp, priority tier, score, SLA pressure, decision id. |
| Honest label | A **diagnostic** of rule fit. | The **operational** recommendation. |
| Use | Ask whether the rules themselves are sensible. | Ask what the team would actually do on Monday. |

The static view is useful and is kept, but on its own it is not executable: because it never consumes capacity, the
same lightly loaded RM can win thousands of times. It must never be described as a capacity-balanced assignment.

### The capacity ledger

The operational model walks the decisions once, in the declared order, and keeps a ledger per RM holding the capacity
snapshot it is based on and the simulated workload added since.

1. Start from the latest valid capacity snapshot at or before the decision instant. Nothing later is read.
2. If that snapshot is newer than the RM's current ledger basis, the observed snapshot **replaces** the basis and the
   simulated additions reset to zero — the observed snapshot already reflects the work the RM is really carrying, so
   continuing to add to the older basis would double-count.
3. Simulated post-assignment workload = observed workload at the basis + simulated additions since + the workload one
   new assignment adds. That last value is **1.00 point**, the weight CAP-01 of the reviewed capacity design gives an
   assigned, not-yet-contacted lead. It is declared in `routing_capacity_ledger_config.csv`, not invented here.
4. Candidates are tried in the declared preference order. The first whose simulated post-assignment workload stays
   **strictly below** the declared limit is chosen — the same strict test REL-13 applies before the assignment, so an
   ordinary assignment never pushes an RM to or past the hard limit.
5. That RM's ledger balance is incremented, and the next decision sees it.
6. If no eligible candidate has capacity, the lead is held in the declared capacity queue, never assigned beyond
   capacity.

Every one of those quantities is published on each row: the snapshot and its timestamp, the observed workload, the
simulated workload already added, the workload this recommendation adds, the simulated post-assignment workload, the
limit and the remaining capacity.

**What the ledger changes in this baseline: 46 decisions.** The rank-1 candidate was skipped for capacity and the lead
went to the next eligible RM. No lead had to be queued for capacity, and the maximum simulated post-assignment
utilization is 0.9938 — the ledger binds, but only just.

## Comparing three policies

The synthetic historical assignments are **not correct labels**. They are the output of one simulated policy; the other
two are the declared rules applied with and without capacity consumption. Differences measure rule adherence and
operational exposure, nothing more. Full period, all 19,777 decisions, all three policies on the identical population.

| Measure | Historical | Static diagnostic | Capacity-aware operational |
|---|---|---|---|
| Assignment rate | 91.45% | 90.76% | 90.76% |
| Queue rate | 8.55% | 9.24% | 9.24% |
| Territory-rule compliance | 98.47% | 100.00% | 100.00% |
| Mandatory-specialist compliance | 90.55% | 100.00% | 100.00% |
| Mandatory-specialist fulfilment | 83.60% | 90.58% | 90.58% |
| Preferred-specialist fulfilment | 10.91% | 8.34% | 8.34% |
| Language fulfilment | 76.74% | 89.01% | 89.01% |
| Hard-capacity breaches | 0.11% | 0.00% | 0.00% |
| Top-decile share of assignments | 33.99% | 72.31% | 72.31% |
| Largest number of assignments to one RM | 663 | 3,234 | 3,216 |
| RMs receiving no assignment at all | 0 | 8 | 8 |
| Workload Gini across all RMs | 0.458 | 0.773 | 0.773 |

### Where the declared rules do better

Territory compliance, specialist compliance and language fulfilment are all higher, and hard-capacity breaches fall to
zero. These are not surprises — they are eligibility rules, so the framework cannot violate them by construction, while
the historical simulation could. The business reading is narrow but real: a rules-driven router removes a class of
avoidable assignment errors that a manual or round-robin process produces.

### Where they do worse, and what that means

**Preferred-specialist fulfilment falls from 10.91% to 8.34%.** RPF-02 weights specialist fit at 0.18 while capacity
headroom and workload balance together carry 0.32, so a preferred-specialist lead often goes to a lighter-loaded
generalist. This is a declared trade-off. It is reported as it stands and was not tuned away after the fact. If the
business judged specialist conversation quality more important than load smoothing, the fix is a weight review with
stakeholders, not a quiet adjustment here.

**Workload concentration is far worse, and the capacity ledger barely improves it.** Gini 0.773 against 0.458; one RM
receives 3,216 assignments where the historical maximum is 663; 8 of 140 RMs receive nothing at all. The ledger moves
only 46 decisions. That is the most important honest finding in this layer, and the reason is structural:

- the capacity snapshots are the ones the **historical** policy produced, and a new observed snapshot arrives nightly;
- the ledger correctly resets to each new observed snapshot, because that snapshot is the evidence of what the RM is
  really carrying;
- so simulated load never carries across a snapshot boundary. Within a single day the ledger binds — the largest
  simulated addition reaches 38 of 40 points — but across a year it cannot.

The business implication is specific: **this layer should not be presented as a workload-balancing solution.** It
enforces capacity limits correctly within the evidence it has, and it demonstrably redirects leads when an RM fills up
inside a snapshot window. But reproducing a realistic year-long load distribution would need a capacity ledger fed by
this policy's own assignments, with leads ageing and closing over time, which this simulation does not model. Until
then, the concentration figures are a warning about the preference weights (RPF-05 and RPF-06 reward the emptiest RM,
and in a lightly loaded year that is almost always the same person), not a measured property of a deployed system.

## Operational outcome in numbers

The capacity-aware recommendation assigns an RM for **17,950** of 19,777 decisions and holds the rest in a controlled
queue. The ledger redirects **46** decisions away from the candidate the static view would have chosen.

## Limitations

**Of the baseline being evaluated** — these are properties of the synthetic data, left exactly as they are:

- Every source lead carries priority band **P2**, so the recommendation cannot be compared against a source band.
- Preferred-specialist mismatch is about **70%** of specialist-need decisions in the baseline, because preferred-industry
  specialists are sparse.
- Capacity starts at zero on 2025-06-01 and builds over the first months; 12 decisions precede the first snapshot
  entirely.
- Some leads remain open at the 2026-05-31 cut-off, so late cohorts are immature by construction.
- Some generator ground-truth labels are partly derived from the generator's own records, so their agreement with
  observable predicates is partly circular. Ground truth is never read by this layer in any case.

**Of this framework:**

- **The capacity ledger binds within a snapshot window, not across the year.** Each new observed capacity snapshot
  replaces the ledger basis and resets the simulated additions, because that snapshot is the evidence of what the RM
  is really carrying. But those snapshots were produced by the *historical* policy, so load created by this policy's
  own recommendations never carries into the next day. The ledger therefore enforces the hard limit correctly inside a
  day — it redirects 46 decisions and reaches 38 of 40 points at its peak — while leaving the year-long concentration
  almost unchanged. A faithful long-run simulation would need capacity snapshots generated from this policy's own
  assignments, with leads ageing, being contacted and closing, which is out of scope here.
- **The assignment workload is a flat one point.** CAP-01 weights an assigned, not-yet-contacted lead at 1.0, and the
  ledger applies exactly that. It does not model the lead later becoming a contacted lead (0.5) or an open opportunity
  (1.5), so the simulated load is the load at the moment of assignment only.
- **The static diagnostic never consumes capacity.** It is published as a diagnostic of rule fit and must not be read
  as an executable plan; the operational mart is the one that can be.
- **The decision timestamps are the historical policy's.** This is a counterfactual at the same instants, not a redesign
  of when decisions are taken. The intake-routability review points are a constructed decision moment.
- **P1 is largely override-driven**: 831 of 2,905 P1 decisions reach the floor on their base score, and reassignment
  decisions account for 1,636 of them, because initial routing happens within minutes of intake and leaves the urgency
  pillar almost flat.
- **Three eligibility outcomes** (`withdrawn`, `terminal`, `not_yet_routable`) and **one queue** (Q-MOR, manager
  override review) are declared, documented and tested but never observed in this baseline. Q-MOR never fires because
  no decision ends with every candidate blocked solely by the exclusion rule, and because the framework never applies a
  manager override itself: an override stays a human control.
- **RPF-07 cannot discriminate between candidates** once REL-11 has run: every eligible candidate already supports a
  stated language, so the component is constant within a decision and only shifts scores between decisions.
- **REL-11 is stricter than the RT-P04 design fallback**, which would assign and flag for interpreter support. Here an
  unsupported stated language produces a Q-LNG queue result instead.
- **RT-E05 self-assignment and RT-P05 named preferred RM are not modelled**, because neither input is available as an
  as-of field on the synthetic lead.
- **RPF-02 ignores the size condition** in SPC-04 and SPC-05, which prefer a specialist only for established (20-499)
  leads.
- **Lead geography, stated interest, consent and prospect attributes are stored as current values** on the synthetic
  source. Every historical routing decision happens after validation passed, so the current value is the validated
  value at that instant; for the constructed intake-review points this is a documented approximation.
- **The candidate universe excludes RMs with no relationship to the lead's territory.** They are counted, not
  enumerated, so a per-RM territory failure row does not exist for them.
- **Normalization uses declared fixed mappings, not population percentiles**, which makes a score meaningful on its own
  but means it is not calibrated to any observed rate.
